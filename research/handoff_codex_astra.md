## 1. The problem, precisely

For $k\ge2$, $r\ge3$: $f(k,r)$ is the largest transversal number $\tau(\mathcal H)$ over $k$-uniform families $\mathcal H$ with **property $(r,2)$**: every subfamily of **at most** $r$ edges is met by two points. Erdős asks whether $f(k,7)=(3/4+o(1))k$. Put $c_7=\limsup_k f(k,7)/k$.

Conventions used throughout: "at most 7" (with "exactly 7", $C_5$ gives $f(2,7)\ge3$). A 7-tuple $E_1,\dots,E_7$ is **not** 2-pierceable iff its seven complements cover all pairs of points iff no two nonempty Venn cells $c,c'\subseteq[7]$ (cell of $x$ = set of edges containing $x$) satisfy $c\cup c'=[7]$ (Lemma 1.1 of the note). Property $(7,2)$ forces $\nu\le2$ (three disjoint edges are a bad triple). Asymptotic statements are about proportional ("unit") sizes with a scale $r\to\infty$.

## 2. What the literature has (the problem page is incomplete)

- Erdős–Hajnal–Tuza, JCTA 58 (1991): property $(p,1)$ (any $p$ edges share a point) gives $\tau\le\lfloor(r+p-1)/p\rfloor$ via the greedy intersection chain (the chain argument is reproduced in Lemma 4.1(c) of the note).
- Erdős–Fon-Der-Flaass–Kostochka–Tuza, Siberian Adv. Math. 2 (1992): introduces $f(k,r)$.
- **Fon-Der-Flaass–Kostochka–Woodall, Discrete Math. 207 (1999) 277–284** (text in `fkw1999.txt`): $\lceil3k/4\rceil\le f(k,7)\le\lceil7k/8\rceil$, upper bound proved for $k\ge8$ (they write $r=8k+s$, $k\ge1$); parity family $\{4m\text{-subsets of a }(7m+1)\text{-set with odd intersection with a fixed }4m\text{-set}\}$ has $\tau=3m+1$ for $m\ge4$, fails for $m=2$, $m=3$ left open. Their Lemma 1 needs a *good triple* (three edges with empty common intersection) with $a_{12}\le4k+\lceil s/2\rceil$, $\max(a_{13},a_{23})\le3k+s/2$, $a_{13}+a_{23}\le5k+s$ ($a_{ij}=|A_i\cap A_j|$); it does **not** assume the family is intersecting. Their Theorem 2, Case 4 implicitly assumes $a_{12}\ge3k$; the missed sub-case (all three intersections $<k$) is covered by Lemma 1 directly, so the theorem is correct.
- Kostochka, Combinatorica 22 (2002): large $p$ (irrelevant). Bucić–Korándi–Sudakov, Combinatorica 41 (2021): general bounds, notation $h_r(k,\ell)$.

So $c_7\in[3/4,7/8]$, open since 1999.

## 3. Established results (rebuild and re-verify; details and proofs in `note_644.md`)

### 3.1 Elementary and exact facts
- **Covering criterion (proved):** $K_N^{(k)}$ has $(7,2)$ iff no seven $(N-k)$-subsets of $[N]$ cover all pairs, i.e. iff the covering number $C(N,N-k,2)\ge8$. **Lemma 2.2 (proved):** seven blocks covering all pairs of an $N$-set include one of size $\ge3N/7$ (point types $t(a)=\{i:a\in P_i\}$ pairwise intersect; either all $|t(a)|\ge3$ or some $t(a)\subseteq\{i,j\}$ forces $|P_i|+|P_j|\ge N$). Hence $K_N^{(k)}$ has $(7,2)$ for all $N<7k/4$, giving $f(k,7)\ge\lceil3k/4\rceil$ in two lines; the Fano blow-up (seven classes, edges = unions of the four classes off a line; any two points lie on a common line) shows failure for $N\ge7k/4+O(1)$. **The value $3/4$ is attained**, so any upper-bound method must be exact at $3/4$ — this kills every "budget" method (see §5).
- Small values: $f(2,7)=2$ (τ-critical graph with $\tau=3$ has $\le\binom{4}{2}=6$ edges, Bollobás); [C] $f(3,7)=3$ on $\le10$ points, $f(4,7)=3$ on $\le8$ points, $f(5,7)\ge5$ ($C(9,4,2)=8$), $f(6,7)\ge5$; **[C] $f(12,7)\ge10$** (FKW parity family at $m=3$ has $(7,2)$: MILP infeasibility 803 s + independent set-cover check). $f(k+1,7)\ge f(k,7)$ (private vertex per edge).
- Blow-ups (replacing points by classes) keep $\tau$ fixed — they do NOT scale $\tau$; parity conditions cannot be stacked (two parities on a $(7m+2)$-set drop $\tau$ back).

### 3.2 Theorem P (proved, LP duality) — the main positive result
*Pattern family*: parts of sizes $x\in\mathbb R^p_{>0}$, edge size $r$, an edge is an $r$-set whose vector of intersection sizes with the parts lies in a closed **convex** admissible set $\mathrm{Adm}\subseteq\{a:0\le a\le x,\sum a=r\}$. $u$ is *free* if no admissible $a\le u$; $\tau^*=\sum x-\sup\{\sum u:u\text{ free}\}$, and $\tau=m\tau^*+O(1)$ at scale $m$.
**Theorem P.** $\tau^*>3r/4\Rightarrow\exists a\in\mathrm{Adm}$ with $a\le\frac47x$, and then seven classes of size $r/4$ with composition $a/4$ on the Fano points give a non-2-pierceable 7-tuple. So $c_7=3/4$ on convex pattern families (which contain every construction ever proposed). Proof: separate $\mathrm{Adm}$ from the down-set $D=\{b\le\frac47x\}$ by $\lambda\ge0$; $\mu=\min_{\mathrm{Adm}}\lambda\cdot a>\frac47\lambda\cdot x$; $G(s)=\max\{\lambda\cdot w:0\le w\le x,\sum w=s\}$ is concave with $G(0)=0$ and $G(r)\ge\mu$, so $G(3r/4)>\frac37\lambda\cdot x$; $u=x-v^*$ is free with $\sum u=\sum x-3r/4$. Non-convex admissible sets are NOT covered; [C] $\sim50{,}000$ non-convex two-/three-part patterns (boxes, unions of two boxes) with $\tau^*/r\ge0.77$ all fail $(7,2)$ — but see the pocket in §3.3 for how coarse grids can lie.

### 3.3 Two disjoint edges ($\nu=2$)
Let $A_1\cap A_2=\emptyset$ be edges; every edge meets $A_1\cup A_2$; any five other edges are pierced by one point of $A_1$ and one of $A_2$ (cross-$(5,1)$); edges disjoint from $A_2$ have $(6,1)$ (any six share a point) and their $A_1$-traces have $(5,1)$, hence a transversal inside $A_1$ of size $\le\lfloor(r+3)/4\rfloor$ (Lemma 4.1).
- **Lemma 4.2 (proved + machine-checked, legal at budget $3r/4$):** if $\tau>T$, $3r/4\le T\le r$, and some edge $A_3$ has $|A_3\cap A_1|,|A_3\cap A_2|\le T-r/2$, then seven explicit edges are not 2-pierceable ($A_4$ avoids $A_{13}\cup Y_4$, $A_5$ avoids $A_{23}\cup Y_5$, $A_6$ avoids $A_{13}\cup(A_4\cap A_2)$, $A_7$ avoids $(A_5\cap A_1)\cup A_{23}$). So at $\tau>3r/4$ every edge has $>r/4$ points in $A_1$ or in $A_2$. FKW's Lemma 1 at $a_{12}=0$ gives only $a_{13}+a_{23}\le r/4$; Lemma 4.2 subsumes it. Slight extension: $a_{13}\le r/4$, $a_{23}\le r/4+a_{13}/2$ works with a 7-edge variant ($A_5$ avoiding $A_{13}\cup A_{23}\cup Y_5$).
- **Two-shape family $F_\alpha$** = $\{A_1,A_2\}\cup\{r\text{-subsets of }A_1\cup A_2\text{ with traces }(\alpha r,(1-\alpha)r)\text{ or mirror}\}$: $\tau=2\alpha r+2$; [C] has $(7,2)$ for all tested $\alpha<1/4$ (0.05…0.24999), fails at $\alpha=1/4$ (bad tuple: $A_2$ + one $(1/4,3/4)$ edge + five $(3/4,1/4)$ edges whose $A_1$-complements are $Z,Z,W,Y,V$ for a quarter-partition $A_1=Z\sqcup W\sqcup Y\sqcup V$).
- **THE POCKET [C] (new, important):** asymmetric two-type families $F_{\{d,c\}}$ (traces $(d,1-d)$ and $(c,1-c)$, $d<1/2<c$) have $\tau^*=1+d-c$, and a thin band $c+d\approx0.945$, $0.2325\le d\le0.249$ (plus its mirror $(d,c)\mapsto(1-c,1-d)$) **has property $(7,2)$**: e.g. $(0.24,0.705)$: $\tau^*=0.535$; $(0.2475,0.7025)$: $0.545$; **$(0.249,0.701)$: $\tau^*=0.548$**. Every tested point with $\tau^*\ge0.55$ fails (600+ points, grids down to $0.001$; logs `logs/nu2_pocket_*.log`). Cross-checked with HiGHS presolve off. Integer-scale MILPs (scale 100/200) time out; the continuous certificate is the asymptotic proof (a rational bad tuple scales to an integer one and conversely, so continuous infeasibility = no bad tuple at any scale). **Consequently the naive conjecture "two disjoint edges $\Rightarrow\tau\le(1/2+o(1))k$" is false already for type-closed families.**
- **Lemma 4.5 (proved):** for two-part type-closed families $F_C$ ($C\subseteq(0,1)$ the set of $A_1$-trace fractions), $\tau^*(F_C)=2-\sup\{u_1+u_2:u_i<1,\ C\cap[1-u_2,u_1]=\emptyset\}$, and if $\tau^*=1/2+\varepsilon$ then $C$ contains $d<1/2<c\le d+1/2-\varepsilon+\delta$ (for every $\delta>0$); so $\sup\tau^*/r$ over this whole class equals $\sigma_2:=\sup\{1+d-c:F_{\{d,c\}}\text{ has }(7,2)\}$ (property $(7,2)$ passes to subfamilies; $1/2\notin C$ by the four-corner tuple).
- **Theorem 4.6 (computer-assisted, exact rational certificate; was completing when this handoff was written — check `logs/nu2_cover_refine2.out` for `DONE ... uncovered slivers: 0`):** $\sigma_2\le0.55$, i.e. every $F_{\{d,c\}}$ with $c\le d+0.45$ fails $(7,2)$. Method: *templates* (a type per edge + a cell support with no covering pair) extracted from MILP solutions; for each, the region of $(d,c)$ where masses $\ge1/1000$ realise it is convex, and an inner polygon is the convex hull of rational points verified exactly (singleton "slack" cells make regions full-dimensional; degenerate templates are dropped); exact adaptive triangulation checks that the closed triangle $\{0.05\le d\le1/2,\ 1/2\le c\le d+0.45\}$ is covered by the union of polygons (`p644_nu2_pattern_thm.py`, `p644_nu2_cover_refine.py`, `p644_cover_check.py`, templates in `logs/nu2_pattern_thm.json`, ~190 templates). Together $0.548\le\sigma_2\le0.55$.
- **Obstruction (important for any proof of the $\nu=2$ bound):** cross-$(5,1)$ alone (7-tuples containing both $A_1,A_2$) does not bound $\tau$ by anything below $2/3$: $F_{0.3}$ satisfies it with $\tau=0.6r$ (cross-$(5,1)$ holds at $\alpha=0.3$, fails at $1/3$). So any proof must use 7-tuples that omit $A_1$ or $A_2$, and there the adversary's defence is a common point outside $A_1\cup A_2$ shared by all its answers (makes those tuples trivially 2-pierceable). Lemma 4.2's tuple contains both $A_1,A_2$ — that is why it closes.
- **Conjecture N′:** two disjoint edges $\Rightarrow\tau\le(\sigma+o(1))k$ for some $\sigma\in[0.548,3/4)$. The $3/4$ version would reduce Problem 644 to intersecting families; it is NOT proved.

### 3.4 The 1999 method as a game; what it can and cannot do
- **Budget game:** if $\tau>T$, every set of $\le T$ points is avoided by some edge. A proof = a script of requests "an edge avoiding $D_j$", $D_j$ built from Venn cells of the edges obtained so far (whole cells, or named subsets of prescribed size), ending in a subfamily of $\le7$ edges that is not 2-pierceable. **Verifier** `p644_strategy2.py` (sparse MILP over atom masses; compact 2-pierceability encoding $v_s\le\sum z$, $u_m\le\sum_{t\supseteq m}v_t$, $p_s\le v_s,\ p_s\le u_{F-s}$, $\sum p\ge1$ per 7-subset; continuous masses = asymptotic; `check_budget` certifies each request stays $\le T$ in all reachable configurations; named subsets have size $\min(\text{prescribed},\text{host})$ with composition chosen by the adversary — sound for the prover). It reproduces the FKW proof exactly (21/21 parameter triples at budget $14/16$, illegal at $13/16$ exactly where the closed form $|B_4|=3r-3T+a_{12}$ predicts). v1 `p644_strategy.py` had a sign bug in pick constraint (2) (fixed 18 Sep); it never produced a false "prover wins".
- **Closed-form limit of the 1999 scheme:** Lemma 1 Case 1 needs $a_{12}\le(4\beta-3)r$, Theorem Case 2 needs $a_{12}\ge(5/4-\beta)r$ ⇒ $\beta\ge17/20$; at $a_{12}=r/2$ it forces $\beta\ge7/8$.
- **[C] Eight-edge win beyond 7/8:** at good-triple parameters $(a_{12},a_{13},a_{23})=(5,2,2)\cdot r/16$, budget $13r/16$, the script `p644_explore8.script8(5,2,2,0,8,'A')` (FKW steps for $A_4$–$A_6$, then $A_7,A_8$ splitting the over-budget set $B_4$) is infeasible in continuous mode with all requests legal; its 7-edge truncation is feasible. Programme for $c_7\le13/16$ on intersecting families: the outer good-triple choice is free (any two edges + a third avoiding their intersection), so a per-cell case tree over the sorted grid of $(a_{12},a_{13},a_{23})$ (680 unit cells at $r=16$) would BE the theorem; `p644_cover.py` closes cells with $a_{12}\le4$ (family L1) and $a_{12}\in[5,7]$ (family S8) at minutes per cell; **cells with $a_{12}\ge r/2$ ("clustered") need a new script family that was not found** (probes in `p644_familyC.py`, `p644_clustered.py`, `p644_s9.py` found only unsound artefacts before the pick fix and nothing after). The $\nu=2$ side of such a theorem is separate (§3.3).
- **This method cannot reach $3/4$** (exact value attained; the tree blows up as $\beta\to3/4$).

## 4. Dead ends and traps (do not repeat)
1. Computing $\tau$ of a pattern at a small scale ($m=8$) has $O(1/m)$ error that made one-sided families look like $\tau/r=1/2$; use scale $\ge48$ or the exact $\tau^*$ formula.
2. Coarse grids (mesh $1/6$–$1/12$) missed a pocket of width $\approx0.02$; sweep parameters finely near suspected boundaries.
3. Cross-$(5,1)$ alone cannot give the $\nu=2$ bound (§3.3).
4. In the budget game the adversary realises **every membership pattern the requests allow** with tiny cells (continuous relaxation), so survival is existential: mass-based search heuristics are useless; only whole-cell avoidances forbid patterns (picks do not, since the adversary chooses their composition). Three CEGAR/beam searches (`p644_nu2_search*.py`) found nothing; a hand-designed 3-tall/4-wide strategy (`p644_nu2_tw.py`) lost to balanced answers plus shared outside points.
5. In the abstract "$\{A_1,A_2\}$ + 5 rectangles" game (adversary answers a request $D_1\times D_2$ with a rectangle of semi-perimeter exactly $r$ placing the excess adversarially) the prover cannot force a cover at budget $3/4$: excess is wasted next to covered area and holes are kept aligned. A static Fano design on $A_1\cup A_2$ needs budget $6r/7$.
6. The $\{A_2\}$+6 and 7-probe tuples need control of shared outside points (later probes avoiding earlier outside parts), which the adversary counters by inflating outside parts — but outside-heavy answers have huge rectangles, so a case split on outside mass is the natural (unfinished) route.
7. Integer MILPs at scales $\ge100$ time out; use the continuous formulation (exact for existence at some scale).
8. Two 13-hour CEGAR SAT runs for $f(4,7)$ on 9–10 points and $f(5,7)$ on 10 points never finished; those sizes need symmetry breaking (nauty) or a different method.
9. Fourier–Motzkin on template systems with slack variables blows up; use LP-guided rational points + exact verification + convex hull instead (already implemented).
10. `pycddlib` does not build on this machine; `pysat` and `scipy` (HiGHS) are available.

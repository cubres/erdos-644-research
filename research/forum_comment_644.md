Some references not on this page, exact small values, and two partial results (a full write-up with proofs and reproducible code is linked; it was prepared with AI assistance and every computer-verified claim is marked as such there).

**1. Literature.** Fon-Der-Flaass, Kostochka and Woodall, *Transversals in uniform hypergraphs with property (7,2)*, Discrete Math. 207 (1999) 277–284, prove $\lceil 3k/4\rceil \le f(k,7)\le\lceil 7k/8\rceil$ (the upper bound for $k\ge 8$) and give a family with $\tau=3m+1$ for $k=4m$, $m\ge4$ (all $4m$-subsets of a $(7m+1)$-set with odd intersection with a fixed $4m$-set), one more than the complete hypergraph. Their argument leaves $m=3$ open; a mixed-integer program on the Venn-cell counts of a hypothetical bad 7-tuple shows that family does have property (7,2), so $f(12,7)\ge 10$. Also relevant: Erdős–Hajnal–Tuza, JCTA 58 (1991) (property $(p,1)$); Erdős–Fon-Der-Flaass–Kostochka–Tuza, Siberian Adv. Math. 2 (1992) (the definition of $f(k,r)$); Bucić–Korándi–Sudakov, Combinatorica 41 (2021) (general bounds, notation $h_r(k,\ell)$). So the first question is open with $c_7\in[3/4,7/8]$.

**2. Complete hypergraphs.** $K^{(k)}_N$ has property (7,2) iff no seven $(N-k)$-subsets of $[N]$ cover all pairs. Seven sets covering all pairs of an $N$-set always include one of size $\ge 3N/7$ (look at the point types $t(a)=\{i:a\in P_i\}$, which pairwise intersect), so $K^{(k)}_N$ has the property for every $N<7k/4$; this gives $f(k,7)\ge\lceil 3k/4\rceil$ in two lines, and the Fano blow-up shows it fails once $N\ge 7k/4+O(1)$. With "at most 7 sets" in the definition (with "exactly 7" the 5-cycle gives $f(2,7)\ge3$): $f(2,7)=2$ (a $\tau$-critical graph with $\tau=3$ has at most 6 edges), $f(3,7)=3$ on $\le10$ points, $f(4,7)=3$ on $\le 8$ points, $f(5,7)\ge5$ since $C(9,4,2)=8$, and $f(k+1,7)\ge f(k,7)$ (add a private vertex to every edge).

**3. Structured families.** Call a family a *pattern family* if the ground set is split into parts and a $k$-set is an edge iff its vector of intersection sizes with the parts lies in a fixed convex set (this covers complete hypergraphs and everything obtained from them by convex intersection conditions). For every such family the answer to the question is yes: if $\tau>3k/4$, an LP-duality argument produces an admissible composition $a\le\frac47 x$, and seven classes of size $k/4$ with composition $a/4$, placed on the points of the Fano plane, give seven edges (the unions of the four classes off each line) which are not 2-pierceable, because any two points lie on a common line. The parity family above is non-convex and gains only an additive constant.

**4. Two disjoint edges.** If the family contains two disjoint edges $A_1,A_2$ (matching number is at most 2 anyway), then: (a) if $\tau>3k/4$, every edge has more than $k/4$ points in $A_1$ or in $A_2$ — an edge with at most $k/4$ in each yields an explicit non-2-pierceable 7-tuple; (b) the family consisting of $A_1$, $A_2$ and all $k$-subsets of $A_1\cup A_2$ meeting them in $\alpha k$ and $(1-\alpha)k$ points (either way round) has property (7,2) for every $\alpha<1/4$ (verified by the same certificate for a dense set of $\alpha$; it fails exactly at $\alpha=1/4$) and $\tau=2\alpha k+2$, so the constant for this class is at least $1/2$; (c) in an enumeration of more than 20,000 pattern families with two disjoint edges nothing exceeds $1/2$. I conjecture that two disjoint edges force $\tau\le(1/2+o(1))k$; even the weaker bound $3/4$ would reduce the question to intersecting families. Note that the cross-piercing property "any five edges are met by one point of $A_1$ and one of $A_2$" alone does not suffice for either (the $\alpha=0.3$ family satisfies it with $\tau=0.6k$).

**5. The 1999 method.** Encoding the 1999 proof as a "budget game" (the prover repeatedly asks for an edge avoiding a set of size $\le\beta k$ built from the Venn cells of the edges found so far, and must end with a bad 7-tuple) and checking it by a mixed-integer program: Lemma 1 there needs $a_{12}\le(4\beta-3)k$ and Case 2 of the theorem needs $a_{12}\ge(5/4-\beta)k$, so no re-parametrisation of that proof gives $\beta<17/20$. An eight-edge variant (splitting the last avoidance across two edges) does beat $7/8$ at one parameter value with all requests within budget $13/16$, so $c_7<7/8$ looks provable by a computer-assisted case tree, but that has not been completed. (A small gap in Case 4 of the published proof — it implicitly assumes $a_{12}\ge 3k$ — is filled by applying Lemma 1 directly to the case where all three intersections are small.)
